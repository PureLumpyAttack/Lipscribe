Lipscribe for your computer

The desktop app reads your lips on your own computer and speaks for you, in a natural voice or your own, straight into Zoom, Google Meet, Teams or Discord as if it were your microphone.

Download for macOS (Apple silicon) Download for macOS (Intel) Download for Windows

Not sure which Mac you have? Apple menu → About This Mac: "Apple M…" is Apple silicon, "Intel" is Intel. All versions are on the releases page.

The model is built in (about 270 MB download), so lip reading works offline and the video never leaves your computer. Natural and cloned voices use the Lipscribe voice service.

Use it as a microphone in calls

  1. macOS: click Add Lipscribe Microphone under the voice settings and enter your password once. Windows: install the free VB-CABLE virtual audio cable and set Play through to CABLE Input.
  2. In Lipscribe, pick a natural voice or set up your own voice. Use Volume to make it louder for the people on the call. Keep Also play on this computer on to hear yourself.
  3. In your call app, choose Lipscribe Microphone (macOS) or CABLE Output (Windows) as the microphone.
  4. Leave Hands-free on and just talk: Lipscribe records while your lips move and speaks each sentence when you pause. Or press ⌘⇧L / Ctrl+Shift+L to record by hand, even while the call app is in front. Turn on View → Keep on top to keep the camera view visible.

The browser demo does the same in Chrome or Edge with a virtual audio cable (BlackHole on macOS), as long as the tab stays open.

First launch

The app isn't signed by Apple or Microsoft yet. macOS: open Lipscribe once; when macOS says it can't verify the developer, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway (only needed the first time). If macOS says the app is damaged (version 0.1.0), run xattr -cr /Applications/Lipscribe.app in Terminal, then open it again. Windows: choose More info → Run anyway. Allow camera access when asked.
